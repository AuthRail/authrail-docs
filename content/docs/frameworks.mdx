A **Framework** brings together duties your organisation wants or needs to meet.
For example, a Framework may include a duty to provide human oversight of an
AI support assistant. Each duty in AuthRail is a **Framework Obligation**: a
statement of who must do what, under which conditions, with its source.

Use a Framework to build a work list grounded in those duties. Your team still
reviews which ones apply and how its own practices address them.

## Build your first obligation work list

### 1. Choose a Framework

Open **Frameworks** in your AuthRail workspace. Use **Managed catalogue** for
Frameworks managed by AuthRail, **Organisation frameworks** for those your team
authors, or **Active** to return to one already in use.

Open the Framework and read its content. Check the edition, called its
**Framework Version**, before choosing **Activate framework**. AuthRail opens
its workspace with sections for Scope, Obligations, Controls and Readiness.
Activation starts the work; it does not mean the organisation meets the duties.

### 2. Explain your organisation's circumstances

Open **Scope**. Scope tells AuthRail which circumstances to use when reviewing
this Framework—for example, what kind of AI work the organisation performs.
Start with the whole organisation. Use a narrower boundary only when the
answers or operating practice genuinely differ.

Answer the questions and review the resulting decisions. Save a draft while
you are collecting answers. Choose **Apply scope** when the review is ready,
then wait for AuthRail to finish applying the assessment.

If you do not know an answer, keep it unknown and investigate. An unanswered
question is not a reason to exclude a duty.

### 3. Review the duties that apply

Open **Obligations** and read a duty with its supporting source.
**Applicability** tells you whether it applies to the circumstances you reviewed.
**Coverage** tells you which approved practices address it.

<ProductShot src="/screenshots/framework-obligations.jpg" alt="Framework Obligations showing applicability, coverage filters and View coverage actions" caption="Review the duty and its applicability before choosing the practice that addresses it. These decisions belong to the sample workspace." />

Search or filter the list, then choose **View coverage** for a duty you need to
address. For the support assistant's human-oversight duty, you would look for
the approved practice describing how sensitive requests receive review.

### 4. Choose one duty to work on

If an applicable duty has no accepted Control connected to it, keep that gap
visible. A **Control** is the practice your organisation relies on to fulfil a
duty. The next guide shows how to describe and approve it.

You should now have a reviewed work list: duties that apply, duties with a
reviewed reason for exclusion and questions that still need answers. Continue
with [Policies and Controls](/docs/policies-controls) for the duty you chose.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about versions and scope history</summary>

A published Framework Version cannot be edited. Your scope and coverage work
stays associated with that edition when another edition becomes available;
review a newer one before adopting it.

Applied scope answers and decisions form a **Scope Snapshot**, the saved basis
for an assessment. Later changes create new reviewed work rather than erasing
that basis. Unsaved draft answers do not contribute to the applied assessment.

If someone changes scope while you are editing, reload before applying your
review. If applying scope fails, resolve the reported error before relying on
the resulting readiness view.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about pausing or reopening work</summary>

**Suspend framework** pauses active work and retains its identity and history.
It is not deletion and does not resolve an existing gap. If you cannot open a
workspace, check the organisation and your access before starting another
activation.

A not-applicable decision needs a reviewed reason. It cannot stand in for
missing information or an unfinished scope question.

</details>
