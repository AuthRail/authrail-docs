A **Framework** brings together duties from a regulation, standard or your own
organisation. Each duty is a **Framework Obligation** that explains who must do
what, in which circumstances, and where that duty comes from. For example, an
organisation using an AI support assistant may need to show how people oversee
sensitive account changes.

Frameworks help you connect those duties to the way your organisation works and
the proof that supports it. You can connect an application to
[Runtime](/docs/concepts) first and add this governance work when you need it.
There is no Framework setup requirement for trying a protected function.

## A worked example: oversight of account changes

The governance guides follow a fictional organisation, Northstar, whose support
assistant can request changes to customer accounts. Northstar authors an
**AI Operations Standard**, publishes version 1, and includes this duty:

> Before an assistant changes a customer's account access, a named authorised
> person must review and approve the requested change. Keep the request,
> decision and outcome so the organisation can check that the process worked.

This is example organisation-owned content, not a claim about a particular law
or a ready-made entry in the managed catalogue. Use your actual source and its
wording when assessing a regulatory or contractual duty. The example will connect
this duty to a Policy, a Control and a completed October review; each guide
explains the part of that relationship it owns.

## Choose the edition you intend to follow

In <a href={AUTHRAIL.clientOrigin}>AuthRail</a>, open **Frameworks** and browse **Managed catalogue** for content maintained by
AuthRail or **Organisation frameworks** for content your team authors. Before
choosing **Activate framework**, read the source, citations, jurisdiction and
effective dates. The published edition is called a **Framework Version**; make
sure it is the one your organisation intends to work against.

Activation opens a workspace for that version, with Scope, Obligations,
Controls and Readiness together. It begins your organisation's assessment and
does not mean every duty applies or has been met. You can return to this
workspace through **Frameworks → Active**.

## Explain where the duties apply

Open **Scope** and answer the questions about your organisation's circumstances.
Start with the whole organisation. A narrower department, team or AI-system
boundary is appropriate when the answers or operating practice differ there.
For the support assistant, the questions might establish what work it performs
and which responsibilities your organisation has.

Review the applicability decisions as well as your answers. **Applicability**
means whether a duty applies in those circumstances. If you do not know an
answer, leave it unknown while you investigate; missing information cannot serve
as a reason to exclude a duty. Save a draft while gathering facts, then choose
**Apply scope** when the review is complete. Required unresolved questions or
decisions must be addressed before it can be applied.

Applying scope saves a **Scope Snapshot**: the answers and decisions used for
that assessment. A saved draft does not change the active snapshot. If applying
scope continues in the background, wait for its result rather than submitting
the same work again.

### Work through the example's applicability

Northstar's published Framework includes questions about using assistants and
letting them change account access. In its whole-organisation Scope, the answers
and the reason for them would be:

| Question in this example | Answer | Reason to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the organisation use an assistant in customer support? | Yes | The support application uses an assistant to handle authenticated customer requests. |
| Can that assistant request a change to customer account access? | Yes | It can request an access change through the application's protected function. |
| Which part of the organisation operates differently? | No separate segment is needed | The same approval process applies wherever this function is used. |

The first two answers support an **applicable** decision for the duty. Explain
that the assistant can request a sensitive change even though a human must approve
it; having a review process does not make the duty inapplicable. If the team has
not yet established what the assistant can reach, leave that answer unresolved
and investigate its tools rather than answering No.

Review the duty's decision and rationale, then apply the completed Scope. Keep
the resulting Snapshot as the assessment's boundary. When a second support team
later uses a genuinely different process, review that difference explicitly;
simply adding another Framework does not create another organisational scope.

## Open a duty and review its coverage

In **Obligations**, read a duty alongside its supporting source. Applicability
explains why it is included or excluded; **Coverage** explains which approved
practices address it. Search or filter the list, then choose **View coverage**
for a duty your team needs to work on.

For a human-oversight duty, look for a **Control** describing how sensitive
requests receive review. Where no suitable Control is connected, the coverage
gap remains visible until your team addresses it. Continue with
[Policies and Controls](/docs/policies-controls) to describe the practice and
explain how it fulfils the duty.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>Changing versions or scope later</summary>

Published Framework Versions cannot be edited. When another edition becomes
available, review it before adopting it; your existing scope and coverage stay
associated with the edition they assessed. A material change in circumstances
also needs a new scope draft and snapshot, so earlier assessments remain
understandable.

If scope changes while you are editing, reload the current revision before
applying your work. Resolve a failed application before relying on readiness.
**Suspend framework** pauses active work while keeping its identity and history;
it does not delete the assessment or resolve its gaps.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>Publishing an organisation-owned Framework</summary>

Your organisation owns the sources and meaning of a Framework it authors. Save
its complete source material, Obligations and scope questions, then follow the
publication review. When an eligible independent reviewer is available, that
review must finish before publication. Otherwise, an authorised sole operator
can publish the saved version directly. An open or rejected review still needs
to be resolved.

AuthRail checks the publisher's access and the saved content when publishing.
AI can help prepare the material, but a person makes the publication decision.
Managed reference content and your own published content do not certify your
organisation's legal compliance.

</details>
