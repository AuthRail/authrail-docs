An AI support assistant can read a conversation, suggest a reply and ask a tool
to change a customer's account. Giving it a useful model is one part of the
work. Your team also needs to decide which changes it may request, when a
person should review them and how to check what happened.

**AuthRail is a workspace for governing how your organisation uses AI.** It
helps you organise the duties you follow, document the practices your teams use,
review proof that those practices work and check actions in connected AI
applications.

You can start with the organisation's governance work. Installing an SDK comes
later, when you connect an application.

## Follow one support-team example

Suppose your support team wants a person to review sensitive account changes
before its AI assistant can make them. The team needs a clear practice, a way to
apply it and records it can review afterward.

### 1. Understand the duties you need to meet

A **Framework** groups duties your organisation follows, such as obligations
from a regulation or standard. In AuthRail, you choose a Framework, explain
where it applies and review the duties relevant to your organisation.

For the support team, one applicable duty might concern human oversight. That
gives the team a reason to establish and check its review process. The
[Frameworks guide](/docs/frameworks) shows how to start that work.

### 2. Describe what people should do

A **Policy** states the organisation's expectation: sensitive account changes
need human review. A **Control** describes the practice that fulfils it: who
reviews the request, what they inspect and how the application waits for their
decision.

The same Policy and Control can support several Frameworks. You maintain the
practice once rather than writing a different copy for each obligation.

### 3. Check that the practice works

A **Test** describes a repeatable check. For example: inspect the support team's
sensitive changes for a month and check whether each has the required review.
**Evidence** is the material reviewed to support the conclusion, such as the
request list and approval records.

**Readiness** shows how that reviewed work supports the Framework's applicable
duties and what still needs attention. A missing record or failed check stays
visible; a percentage is not legal certification.

### 4. Apply the rule inside an AI application

**Runtime** connects reviewed rules to an application's actual tools. Those
rules are called **Rails**. They can check a request before a tool runs and
check its result before information returns to the agent.

For the support assistant, Runtime can require approval before the protected
account-change function continues. Your application keeps the provider
credentials and performs the operation. AuthRail records the decisions and the
application's execution reports so the team can investigate them afterward.

Runtime has two modes. **Observe** shows what the rules would decide while the
application continues. **Enforce** makes the connected protected path require
permission before it runs. The developer must place the check at the real tool
and keep the agent from reaching its credentials another way.

## Start with your organisation's work

Begin with [Frameworks and scope](/docs/frameworks), then follow the governance
guides through Policies, Controls and proof. You will have a clear account of
what your team expects, how it operates and what supports that account.

After that, [How Runtime works](/docs/concepts) explains the application
connection. The [Runtime walkthrough](/docs/application-example) shows a sample
request, the running sidecar and the resulting AuthRail records.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about decisions, execution and proof</summary>

An allowed decision says that a request met the checked rules. An execution
report says what the application reported about its attempt. Looking up the
provider's own record can establish whether an external change happened.
These are different questions, and AuthRail keeps their records separate.

An **Action** brings together a Runtime request's checks, approval and execution
reports. **Activity Records** describe meaningful activity for review. A signed
record helps establish integrity; it does not independently prove an external
effect or certify compliance.

Runtime records can support a Control's Test or Evidence review when that
review defines which requests and outcomes qualify. They do not automatically
make a Framework obligation ready.

</details>
