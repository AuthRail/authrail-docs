A **Policy** says what your organisation expects. A **Control** describes how
people and systems put that expectation into practice.

For example, a support Policy may require human review before sensitive account
changes. Its Control explains who reviews a request, what they check, how the
application waits and where the decision is saved. Use these records when you
need a practice that a team can follow and a reviewer can inspect.

## Document one review practice

### 1. Write the expectation in a Policy

Open **Policies** and prepare the organisation's document. Give it a responsible
owner, an audience and a review schedule. State which requests need human
review and why. Policy Library material can provide a starting point, but your
organisation still reviews and publishes its own document.

Ask the authorised reviewers to review it. Publication keeps the agreed
edition available for the people and Controls that rely on it.

<ProductShot src="/screenshots/policy-published-version.jpg" alt="Published Policy showing its purpose, owner, audience and review dates" caption="The Policy records the expectation and who is responsible for maintaining it." />

### 2. Describe the working process in a Control

Open **Controls** and describe the practice that fulfils the expectation. For
the support review process, include:

- **Outcome:** sensitive account changes receive the required human review.
- **Responsibility:** who maintains the process and who reviews requests.
- **Procedure:** what the reviewer inspects and how the application waits for
  the decision.
- **Verification:** how the team will check the requests and saved approvals
  over a defined period.

If the practice relies on a Policy, select the published Policy version that
supplies the expectation. Review and approve the Control when it accurately
describes the work.

<ProductShot src="/screenshots/control-approved-with-draft.jpg" alt="Control showing an approved practice, its maintainer and separate draft changes" caption="An approved Control is the practice in use. Separate draft changes still need review before replacing it." />

### 3. Connect the practice to the duty it addresses

Return to the active Framework's **Obligations** and choose **View coverage**
for the relevant duty. Select or review the approved Control that addresses
it. The Framework's **Controls** section also offers **Choose Controls for
Obligations** and **Create Control** when your access permits them.

This reviewed connection is called a **Mapping**. It records which approved
practice your organisation relies on for that applicable duty. Explain how the
practice addresses the duty; a similar title alone is not enough.

You should now have a published expectation, an approved working practice and a
reviewed connection to the obligation. Next, [Tests and Evidence](/docs/tests-evidence)
shows how to check whether the practice works.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about reusing Controls and Policies</summary>

Policies and Controls belong to the organisation. Reuse one approved practice
when several Frameworks call for the same process. Creating a copy for each
Framework makes it harder to maintain one reliable account of the work.

A scoped **Implementation** describes a genuine variation of a Control. For
example, a separate support operation may have a different reviewer and
procedure. Use an Implementation when that difference matters, not simply
because the Control supports another Framework.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about approval and later changes</summary>

A draft and an AI suggestion still need human review. A published Policy or
approved Control version preserves the edition that was reviewed. Later edits
do not overwrite what was approved before.

A Mapping preserves the obligation's applicability decision and the selected
approved Control version. A draft, a free-text Policy mention or a newer Control
version does not silently replace that reviewed connection. Review a replacement
when the duty or practice changes.

If a Control cannot be selected, check its approval state, audience and your
access. If several Controls contribute, review their combined coverage rather
than assuming one partial practice fulfils the whole duty.

</details>
