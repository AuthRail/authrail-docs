A **Policy** states what your organisation expects. A **Control** explains how
people and systems carry out that expectation. A published Policy supplies the
authority for a Policy Rail; the Rail applies its rules at a connected action.
A Control documents the practice and how the team checks it. You can publish a
Policy and use it in Runtime without activating a Framework.

For example, a support Policy may require human approval before sensitive
account changes. The Control describes who reviews a request, what they
inspect, how the application waits and where the decision is saved. The same
practice can later support several Frameworks without becoming a separate
document for each one.

## Follow one practice from expectation to coverage

Suppose Northstar wants human review before its support assistant changes
customer account access. The team will write **Human oversight of account
changes**, a Policy, and describe its operating practice in **Review assistant
account-access changes**, a Control. These are names for the worked example;
your documents should describe the people and systems your organisation uses.
The Policy can govern the connected action through a [Policy Rail](/docs/rails).
The later Framework section shows how to reuse the practice for an assessment.

## Write the expectation

In <a href={AUTHRAIL.clientOrigin}>AuthRail</a>, open **Policies** and prepare your organisation's document. Explain which
requests need review, why the review matters and who is responsible for it.
Choose the audience, owner and review schedule so people know whether the
Policy applies to them and who maintains it.

For Northstar's Policy, write the expectation in terms a support colleague can
use:

> The support assistant may prepare an account-access change, but the application
> must hold it until a named support lead approves the same customer, change and
> purpose. Rejected or expired requests must not execute. The application must
> retain the reviewed request and the outcome of any attempted change.

Set the owner to the person accountable for customer support and include the
support and engineering teams in the appropriate audience. A quarterly review
can check whether responsibilities and supported account changes have changed;
it does not replace checking individual requests when they occur.

The Policy Library can provide starting material. Adopt an entry through
**Add to Policies**, then adapt the resulting draft to your organisation's
systems and responsibilities. Library updates will not change your document
later without your team's involvement.

Save the complete draft before asking for review. When eligible independent
reviewers are available, assign them and complete their review; an authorised
publisher can then choose **Publish version**. If no independent reviewer is
available, an authorised sole operator can publish directly. AuthRail checks
that this option is still permitted, and it cannot bypass a review that is
already open or rejected.

## Describe how the team follows it

Open **Controls** and write the process that puts the Policy into practice.
For the support example, explain which changes require approval, who reviews
them and what happens while the application waits. Include the records the
team keeps and how someone will check the process over a defined period. A
colleague should be able to follow this description without asking its author
to fill in the missing steps.

The Control should turn that expectation into an operating procedure. For this
example, its description can explain the following sequence in connected prose:
the application authenticates the requester and finds the customer account;
AuthRail holds the protected access-change request for the named support lead;
the lead reviews the customer, exact change and reason; the application resumes
the saved request only after approval; and it records the result. A rejected,
expired or uncertain request stays with the support team for the appropriate
follow-up instead of being replaced silently.

Also describe the monthly check: reconcile the application's account-change
history with the saved requests, approvals and execution reports, and investigate
any difference. That comparison matters because reviewing only AuthRail's records
cannot reveal a route that never passed through AuthRail. The
[Tests and Evidence example](/docs/tests-evidence) supplies the period and pass
criteria for this check.

Before approving Northstar's Control, add the monthly Test and Evidence
requirement described in [Tests and Evidence](/docs/tests-evidence). They are part
of the version the Mapping will rely on. If the process relies on a Policy,
select its published version and the sections that govern it. Save the Control draft and choose **Submit for review** when
independent reviewers are available. Its author cannot review the same draft in
that round. Where no eligible independent reviewer exists, an authorised
manager with review access can use **Approve Control** instead.

## Connect the Control to a Framework duty

When the organisation also needs a compliance assessment, it can reuse this
Policy and Control. The [Frameworks and scope example](/docs/frameworks) creates
Northstar's AI Operations Standard version 1 with an obligation requiring this
same before-change review.

Return to the active Framework's **Obligations** and choose **View coverage**
for the duty it addresses. Select the approved Control and explain how its
practice fulfils that duty. This connection is a **Mapping**: it records the
applicability decision, the Control version your organisation relies on and
when the connection takes effect.

For Northstar, select the approved version of **Review assistant account-access
changes** against the applicable duty in AI Operations Standard version 1. In the
Mapping rationale, explain:

> This Control implements the duty's before-change review through a named
> approver and a saved request. Its monthly Test reconciles account changes with
> those approvals, and its required Evidence preserves the review material.
> Coverage is limited to the account-access function and application described
> by the Control; other assistant tools need their own coverage assessment.

Review the selected applicability decision, Control version and effective date
before accepting the Mapping. Then review the collective coverage of the duty.
Do not mark it fully covered if the duty also requires a practice this Control
does not address. An accepted Mapping identifies what will be assessed; it does
not say that the Test has passed or its Evidence has been accepted.

Several Controls may contribute to one duty. Review whether they cover it
together and record your reasoning; similar titles alone do not establish
coverage. Adding or withdrawing a Mapping changes that basis, so the coverage
review needs to be repeated. The Framework's **Controls** area also offers
**Choose Controls for Obligations** and **Create Control** when your access
permits them.

With the practice approved and its relationship to the duty established,
continue to [Tests and Evidence](/docs/tests-evidence). That is where you define
how to check whether the practice works.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>When a team operates the Control differently</summary>

Controls normally describe organisation-wide practice. Add a scoped
**Implementation** only when a department, team or AI system operates it
differently—for example, when a separate support operation uses a different
review procedure. Describe that difference and complete its review. A second
Framework or a different owner alone does not require another Implementation.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>Maintaining published Policies and approved Controls</summary>

Later edits create drafts rather than replacing what was approved. A Mapping
continues to refer to its selected Control version until you review a
replacement. When that Control requires a Policy to remain current, expiry,
archive or supersession can affect its Health until the Policy relationship is
updated and the replacement Control is approved.

Policy renewal begins a draft from a published edition. Review its audience,
responsibilities and due date along with the text. Unpublishing a Library entry
stops new adoptions but leaves existing organisation Policies and their history
intact.

To investigate a possible contradiction, open **Policies → Conflicts** and read
both cited passages, audiences and effective dates. Confirm or dismiss it with
a reason another person can follow. AI suggestions require this human review;
a Policy conflict has its own workflow rather than automatically becoming a
Framework Finding.

</details>
