**Readiness** shows whether the duties that apply to your organisation have the
approved practices and supporting proof they need. It helps you understand the
work behind an assessment and decide what to address next.

For example, a support team's human-review process may be approved while its
monthly Test finds changes without approval records. Readiness follows that
failure back to the affected duty. The percentage comes from these underlying
records; you cannot edit it, and it is not legal certification.

## Find what is holding a duty back

Open the active Framework's **Overview** or **Readiness**. Read the assessment
time and state counts, then choose **View obligation readiness** to see the
duties behind the total. Open a duty marked **Not ready** or **Unknown** and
follow its explanation to the relevant Control, Policy, Test, Evidence or
coverage decision.

AuthRail calls the assessment of a Control in that relationship **mapped
Health**. A Control may support several duties, so its overall summary is not
enough to explain each one. Read the Health and proof attached to the Mapping
you are investigating.

## Read the worked example's assessment

Follow Northstar's example through [Frameworks and scope](/docs/frameworks),
[Policies and Controls](/docs/policies-controls) and
[Tests and Evidence](/docs/tests-evidence). At **2 November 2026 at 12:00 UTC**,
open its account-change oversight duty and check each reason behind its result:

| Assessment input | What is in place in the example |
| --- | --- |
| Applicability | The accepted decision says the duty applies to the whole organisation, using the applied Scope Snapshot. |
| Coverage | The accepted Mapping selects the approved account-change Control Version, with the collective coverage review complete. |
| Policy basis | The selected human-oversight Policy version remains valid for that Control. |
| Test | The October review passed at 10:00 UTC, with the approved procedure, period and freshness. |
| Evidence | The October review package was accepted for the matching requirement and remains available and current. |

When those are all the Mapping's required inputs and no other blocker remains,
its Health can be **Healthy** and the duty **Ready**. Merely uploading the package
would leave the Evidence requirement unsatisfied until the Record was accepted.
A passed Test against a different Control Version would also fail to supply this
Mapping's input, even if it had the same title.

Now keep the same example without adding fresh proof. The Evidence is due on
**1 December 2026 at 00:00 UTC**; the Test is due on **2 December at 10:00 UTC**.
A due input is already a known blocker in mapped Health, so ordinary Ready
status cannot continue past the first due deadline without suitable new proof.
The freshness limits are separate outer age limits: at **16 December 2026 at
00:00 UTC**, the Evidence becomes stale. The old Test has not yet reached its
own maximum age, but it is due and cannot substitute for the required Evidence.
The Test itself becomes stale on **17 December at 10:00 UTC**. Read the live explanation to see each input rather than treating
one green summary as a permanent result.

These dates illustrate the settings chosen for the worked example. Your page
uses your saved times, required periods and definitions. A Framework with other
unmet duties remains not ready overall even when this one duty is Ready. If an
assessment cannot establish a required fact, investigate its Unknown explanation
instead of assuming the missing information is a failure or a pass.

## Address the problem at its source

For the missing-approvals example, investigate the affected requests and fix the
review or record-keeping process. Then perform the appropriate Test and submit
its supporting proof. The previous failure stays in history so someone reviewing
the period can understand both the gap and what changed.

A **Finding** records the problem; a **Task** organises the work to address it.
Open the Framework's **Findings** view, read the source and history, and assign
Tasks with a clear outcome, owner and due date. Completing a Task records that
work as done. The owning Test, Evidence or other source still needs to record
its resolution before the Finding can close.

For example, if the October review found one account change without approval,
record the failed Test and retain the unmatched change in its source material.
A remediation Task could ask engineering to close the unprotected route and
support to review the affected account. Completing that Task does not change
October's failed check. Perform and record a suitable later Test after the fix,
submit the proof it requires, and let those source records establish the new
state. A temporary Exception, where eligible, records acceptance of the gap; it
does not replace the missing approval in the original record.

Return to Readiness after updating those records and check the new assessment
time and remaining blockers. If scope is still being applied, wait for it to
finish before relying on the assessment.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>How the assessment is calculated</summary>

Readiness follows the source of the duty through to the proof for the practice
that addresses it:

```text
authoritative source
  → immutable Framework Version
  → Framework Obligation
  → accepted Applicability Decision
  → accepted Obligation-Control Mapping
  → approved Control Version
  → optional scoped Implementation Version
  → Tests and Control-owned Evidence
  → mapped Health
  → Obligation Readiness
  → Framework Readiness
```

Each relationship identifies the versions used in the assessment. Linking an
Action, Policy or file for context does not replace the Mapping or its required
proof. Only Health for that Mapping contributes to the duty; aggregate Control
Health is a summary across its relationships.

Mapped Health is **Healthy**, **Unmet**, **Unknown** or **Not evaluated**. A known
failure or missing proof is unmet, indeterminate information is unknown, and
empty Test and Evidence expectations are not evaluated. Obligation Readiness
then uses the following states:

| State | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Ready | The required coverage and current proof are in place. |
| Ready with exceptions | An approved Exception covers an eligible known blocker for its effective period. |
| Not ready | A known blocker needs attention. |
| Unknown | The available information does not support a dependable conclusion. |
| Not applicable | A reviewed applicability decision excludes the duty for this scope. |

A Framework uses the first four states. Its percentage counts **Ready** and
**Ready with exceptions** against the current applicable duties. Duties found
not applicable are outside that total; unknowns do not become successes. A
positive result below one percent appears as `<1%`. The assessment also keeps
its evaluation time and inputs so you can explain what the result relied on.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>Accepting a gap temporarily with an Exception</summary>

An **Exception** records a decision to accept a specific eligible gap for a
limited time. Where Readiness offers **Request exception**, describe the reason,
owner, expiry and measures that reduce risk while the gap remains. Choose
**Submit for approval** for the authorised independent reviewer to consider it.

An approval may make the duty **Ready with exceptions** while the Exception is
in effect. It does not turn a failed source fact into a healthy one, and it
cannot resolve unknown applicability, incomplete coverage or mismatched versions
and boundaries. Once it expires, that approval no longer supports the result.
The requester's approval cannot stand in for independent review.

</details>

## Save an assessment for a later review

Open **Audit reports** in the Framework workspace and choose **Create report**
when the current state represents the review point you want to preserve. The
report captures scope, duties, Mappings, Controls, Policies, proof, Findings,
Tasks and Exceptions as they were then. Later changes to live readiness do not
rewrite it; create a new report for another review date.

Creating a report also starts its first download. When the archive is ready,
choose **Download report**. If it fails to prepare or expires, **Prepare
download** creates another archive from the same report. Preparation runs in
the background, so follow its status while it finishes. You need organisation
data-export permission and a recent sign-in to create or download reports;
follow the sign-in prompt and return to the report when asked.

Personal and organisation data exports are available separately through the
account menu under **Settings → Exports**. They have their own scope and access
checks and do not replace a Framework's point-in-time report.
