**Readiness** shows which applicable Framework duties have the approved
practices and supporting proof they need. Use it to find the next piece of
work, not to assign your organisation a score.

For example, the support team's human-review practice may be approved, but its
monthly Test found requests without approval records. Readiness helps you
follow that gap back to the failed check and the work needed to resolve it.
The assessment is derived from those records; you cannot edit the percentage.

## Find and resolve one blocked obligation

### 1. Open the Framework's assessment

Open the active Framework's **Overview** or **Readiness**. Read the overall
state, assessment time and obligation counts. Choose **View obligation
readiness** to see the duties behind the total.

<ProductShot src="/screenshots/framework-readiness.jpg" alt="Framework readiness showing Not ready, one of 217 obligations ready and separate assessment states" caption="The sample has one ready obligation. The remaining states identify the work behind the percentage." />

### 2. Choose a duty that needs attention

Open an obligation marked **Not ready** or **Unknown** and read its blockers.
Follow the connected Control and its Tests and Evidence. A missing approval,
failed check or expired piece of proof needs a different response from an
unanswered scope question.

AuthRail calls the assessment of that mapped practice **Health**. For this
review, use the Health and proof connected to the obligation you opened, rather
than assuming a Control's overall summary answers every duty it supports.

### 3. Address the cause

If a Test found missing approval records, investigate the affected requests.
Fix the review or record-retention process, then perform the appropriate check
and submit the supporting proof. Keep the earlier failure in history.

A **Finding** records a problem that needs attention. A **Task** organises work
to address it. Use the Framework's **Findings** view to follow the problem and
its work. Finishing a Task alone does not rewrite a failed Test or close its
Finding; the source problem has its own review and closure process.

### 4. Check the updated assessment

Return to Readiness after the reviewed records have been updated. Check its
assessment time and remaining blockers. If scope changes are still being
applied, wait for that work to finish before relying on the result.

You should be able to explain why the duty is ready or what still blocks it,
using the records behind the answer. Readiness is an operational assessment,
not legal certification. Continue to [Runtime](/docs/concepts) when you are
ready to connect these expectations to an application's actions.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about readiness states and percentages</summary>

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ready | Required coverage and usable supporting proof are in place. |
| Ready with exceptions | An approved, effective Exception covers an eligible known blocker within its limits. |
| Not ready | A known blocker needs work, such as incomplete coverage, a failed check or missing proof. |
| Unknown | The information does not support a dependable conclusion. |
| Not applicable | A reviewed applicability decision excludes this duty for the assessed scope. |

The percentage counts Ready and Ready with exceptions against the assessed
applicable obligations. Not-applicable obligations are outside that total;
unknowns do not silently become successes. A positive result below one percent
is shown as less than one percent.

A passing Test and accepted Evidence support the practice and period assessed.
They do not automatically address another obligation or a different practice
version. The overall Control summary cannot replace the assessment for the
reviewed obligation-to-Control Mapping.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about Exceptions</summary>

An **Exception** records a decision to accept a specific eligible gap for a
limited time. It names the blocker, explains the reason and boundary, sets an
expiry, and records steps to reduce risk while the gap exists.

Where Readiness offers **Request exception**, select the blocker and provide
that context. **Submit for approval** sends it to an authorised reviewer. A
requester does not independently approve their own request.

An Exception does not make a failed source fact healthy. It cannot resolve
unknown applicability, incomplete coverage or mismatched versions and
boundaries. After it expires, the earlier approval is no longer a basis for
relying on a with-exceptions assessment.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about saving an assessment for review</summary>

Use **Audit reports** in the Framework workspace to capture its scope, coverage,
proof and assessment at a point in time. The report keeps that saved view while
live readiness continues to change.

Archive preparation and download have their own status and access checks. If
preparation fails or a link expires, use the available recovery action. Access
to a report also depends on access to the records it contains.

</details>
