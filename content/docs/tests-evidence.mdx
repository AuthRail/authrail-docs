A **Test** describes a repeatable check of a Control. **Evidence** is the material
reviewed to support the conclusion.

Suppose the support team requires approval before sensitive account changes.
A useful Test checks the team's changes for a month and verifies that each
included request has its required approval. The request list and review records
are supporting Evidence. Use this flow when you need to show that a documented
practice actually worked during a particular period.

## Check one period of human reviews

### 1. Open the Control and define the check

From the active Framework's **Controls**, open the relevant Control's **Tests
and Evidence**. This keeps the check connected to the obligation you are
investigating. You can also reach it from the Control itself.

While editing the Control draft, choose **Add test** in **Tests**. Describe the
procedure, what would count as a pass and which requests are included. Set the
schedule and how long a result may be relied on. A check of one team's requests
must not imply that the whole organisation was inspected.

### 2. State what proof is needed

In **Required Evidence**, use **Add requirement** in the same draft. Describe
the material needed, its source, the period it must cover, how often it should
be supplied and who should review it.

The requirement might ask for a Test result, an uploaded file or an eligible
record from a connected system. Review and approve the Control version with
these instructions before recording results against it.

### 3. Perform the check and record what you found

Inspect the requests and approvals for the period you defined. Choose **Record
Test result**, then enter the period, the material inspected, the outcome and
your explanation. Use **History** to inspect earlier checks.

<ProductShot src="/screenshots/framework-tests-evidence.jpg" alt="Control Tests and Evidence showing a quarterly check, its procedure and Record Test result and History actions" caption="Define the procedure and how long a result remains usable before recording another result." />

Record a failure when the expectation was not met. If the material could not be
retrieved or the check could not complete, record that problem rather than
calling it a pass.

### 4. Submit the supporting Evidence

Use the submission action shown for the requirement, such as **Select Test
result** or **Upload Evidence**. Submit the material for review. An authorised
reviewer checks that it matches the requirement and period, then accepts or
rejects it.

You should now have a saved check and the supporting material with its review
state. Readiness can use eligible results and accepted proof; recording a Test
result does not automatically accept it as Evidence. Continue with
[readiness and remediation](/docs/readiness) to inspect the effect on a duty.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about result names, freshness and history</summary>

A **Test Run** is one recorded check, including its period, inputs, outcome and
explanation. An **Evidence requirement** describes the proof needed; an
**Evidence Record** is one submission with its source and review history.

These records belong to the reviewed Control version. When a scoped
Implementation defines a different practice, define its Tests and Evidence on
that Implementation instead.

A later failed check cannot be hidden by an older pass. A successful result
stops counting when its freshness expires—the date after which a new result
is needed. Replacing Evidence keeps the previous submission and review in
history rather than overwriting them.

Missing material needs a submission; rejected material needs the review reason
addressed; stale material needs a new suitable period. Defining no Tests or
Evidence requirements does not establish that a Control is healthy.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about Evidence review</summary>

File Evidence requires review. If no eligible independent reviewer exists,
AuthRail can accept eligible non-file Evidence under its recorded sole-operator
fallback. Inspect the actual acceptance decision and reason; that fallback is
not independent review.

A Test result required as Evidence still needs a separate Evidence submission
and acceptance. Check source, period, freshness and review state when deciding
which material to rely on. Inaccessible records need the right access; access
failure is not a passing result.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>More about connected records and Runtime proof</summary>

A **Connection** collects read-only records from a provider account your
organisation has authorised. An **Observation** is a saved source fact collected
at a particular time. It can supply an input to a Test or Evidence submission;
it is not itself a passing Test.

Runtime Actions and execution reports can also support a suitably defined
Test. The Test must specify the operations, period and required records.
Observe-mode reports cannot establish enforcement, and a successful Action does
not automatically satisfy the Control. See [Runtime and Framework proof](/docs/framework-proof).

If collection fails, recover the input or replace it with eligible reviewed
proof. Do not turn an unavailable provider record into a passing declaration.

</details>
