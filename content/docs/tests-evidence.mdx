A **Test** describes a repeatable check of a Control. **Evidence** is the
material reviewed to support the conclusion. Together, they help you move from
“this is our process” to “this is what we checked and found.”

Suppose your support team requires approval before sensitive account changes.
A monthly Test could inspect the team's changes and check that each included
request has its approval. The request list and review records would support
the conclusion. The period and team matter: a check of one month's work cannot
establish what happened everywhere or at another time.

## Define the check and the proof it needs

Open the active Framework's **Controls**, then the relevant Control's **Tests
and Evidence** area. Starting here keeps the duty and Mapping visible while
you work. You can also reach the same checks from the Control itself.

In the Control draft, choose **Add test** and describe the procedure, the
requests included and what would count as a pass. Choose how often the check
should happen and how long a result remains usable. Under **Required Evidence**,
choose **Add requirement** to explain the material a reviewer should receive,
its source and period, and the acceptance criteria. Depending on the requirement,
that material may be a file, Test result, provider record or attestation.

Save and approve the Control version with these instructions before recording
results against it. If a scoped Implementation owns a different practice, its
Tests and Evidence belong there instead.

### Configure the account-change review

For Northstar's Control draft from [Policies and Controls](/docs/policies-controls),
choose **Review evidence** when adding the Test. This is a person-led comparison
of records, rather than an automatic assertion that every allowed Action was
properly approved. Give it the name **Monthly account-change approval review**
and use this procedure:

> Obtain the application's complete account-access change history for the review
> period and reconcile it with its saved assistant requests. For every completed
> change, match the customer, change, requester, approval and execution attempt.
> Confirm the named support lead approved that same request before it ran.
> Check that rejected and still-waiting requests produced no change. Investigate
> changes with no matching AuthRail request and requests with an uncertain effect.

Obtain the change history from the system that owns customer account access,
such as its authoritative access-change audit trail. A chat transcript or the
assistant's success message is not that source. Keep enough references in the
review package to let another reviewer repeat the comparison without exposing
private customer data to a wider audience.

Set **Repeat every (days)** to `30` and **Result stays current (days)** to `45`
under **Custom schedule**. The repeat interval tells the owner when another
check is due; a due result is already an unmet input in mapped Health. The
freshness limit gives its maximum age before it becomes stale. Choose these
values for your own risk and operating cycle rather than copying them as an AuthRail default.

Define a file Evidence requirement called **Account-change review package**.
Ask for the period's account-change export, the matched request and approval
references, and the reviewer's reconciliation and exceptions. Choose **Evidence
file** as the proof source. Set **Minimum period covered (days)** to `31` for this example, the cadence to `30` and freshness to `45`.
The acceptance criteria should require complete period coverage, matching
customer/change references and an explanation for every discrepancy. Approve the
Control version containing both definitions before recording the results.

If you add these definitions after a Control Version has already been approved
and mapped, work in a new draft and complete its approval. Review and accept the
replacement Mapping to that new approved version before expecting its results
to affect the duty. The earlier Mapping does not follow your edit automatically.
For a new Control, define its Tests and Evidence in the initial draft, approve
that version and then accept its Mapping as described in
[Policies and Controls](/docs/policies-controls).

## Perform the Test

Inspect the requests and approvals for the period you defined, then choose
**Record Test result**. Enter the period, material inspected, outcome and your
explanation. **History** shows earlier checks so you can understand how the
practice has changed over time.

If approvals are missing, record the failure. If you could not retrieve the
records or finish the check, record that problem rather than a pass. A newer
failure remains relevant even if an older check passed, and a result stops
counting as current when its freshness period ends.

### Record the October result

Use the completed interval **1 October 2026 00:00 UTC to 1 November 2026
00:00 UTC**, covering all of October. In this illustrative review, the operator
performs the Test on **2 November 2026 at 10:00 UTC** and finds:

| Included requests | What the review establishes |
| --- | --- |
| 12 completed account changes | Each has a matching request and named approval earlier than the execution, with the same customer and change. |
| 2 denied requests | Neither has a resulting account change. |
| 1 request still waiting at the period boundary | It has not executed. |
| Application changes without matching requests | None found within the reviewed function and period. |

Record **Pass** only after inspecting the underlying records and explaining
these results. These numbers are example findings, not an expected result to
copy into your own Test. One missing approval would be a failure against this
procedure. An inaccessible application ledger would prevent the comparison and
must not become a pass merely because the AuthRail list looks complete.

At 2 November 10:00 UTC, that passing result is current. With these settings it
is due again on **2 December 2026 at 10:00 UTC** and becomes stale on **17 December
2026 at 10:00 UTC** if it has not been replaced by suitable later proof. A due
result no longer supplies a current passing input, even before it becomes stale. Both times follow the recorded time of performance, not the moment
someone later opens the page.

## Prepare a file and submit it for review

For file Evidence, open **Evidence → Add Evidence**. Give the package a title,
the period it covers and at least one approved Control Version, then choose
the source files. Your browser uploads them directly to private object storage.
Wait for verification and preparation to finish and inspect the source. Any
AI-suggested claims can help you review it, but accepting a claim does not accept
the file as proof for a Control.

Return to the Control's **Tests and Evidence** area. Under **Required Evidence**,
open the requirement's **Upload Evidence** action and select the verified file.
Supply the completed period and collection time, then choose **Submit for
review**. This creates an **Evidence Record** for that requirement. A reviewer
other than the submitter checks whether the source meets the requirement and
accepts or rejects the Record in its history.

For the example, title the package **October account-change review**, associate
it with the same approved Control Version, and submit it to **Account-change
review package** for the completed October interval. Record its actual collection
time, **2 November 2026 at 10:00 UTC**, rather than the upload time if they differ.
The independent reviewer checks all 15 requests and the reconciliation, including
the evidence that the two denials and the held request did not change an account.
Accept only if the package meets the approved requirement; otherwise reject it
with the missing or contradictory material identified.

Evidence freshness is measured from the earlier of its collection time and
period end. This example's accepted package therefore becomes stale on
**16 December 2026 at 00:00 UTC**, before the Test result's own freshness ends.
Uploading the same old package again cannot extend what period it demonstrates.

The uploaded package and the submitted Record serve different purposes. The
package makes the source available; the Record says which requirement and
period it is offered to satisfy. Only accepted, current Evidence that meets
that requirement can contribute to the Control's assessment.

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>Using a Test result, provider record or attestation</summary>

When the requirement expects another source, use **Select Test result**,
**Select provider record** or **Record attestation**. A Test result still needs
this separate submission and acceptance when it is required as Evidence.
Provider records come from [Connections](/docs/connections); select the monitored
Resource and the Observation collected for it. An Observation records a fact
at its collection time, so it cannot by itself represent a month of checks.

File submissions always need independent review. Where no eligible independent
reviewer exists, AuthRail may accept suitable non-file Evidence under its
sole-operator fallback. The saved decision explains when that happened; it
should not be described as independent review.

</details>

<details className="guide-details">
<summary>Keeping proof current and correcting a submission</summary>

A **Test Run** is one recorded check, including its inputs, period and outcome.
An Evidence Record keeps the source, period and review history of one submission.
Both remain associated with the Control or Implementation version they assess.

When proof changes, replace the accepted Evidence for the same requirement and
period through the replacement flow. Earlier submissions and decisions remain
in history. Removing a source does not erase its previous use, but unavailable
material cannot support a new accepted submission or preview.

A rejection needs its review reason addressed; stale proof needs a suitable new
submission. If access or collection fails, resolve that problem before relying
on the material. Having no Test or Evidence expectations does not establish that
a Control is healthy.

</details>

## Follow the result into readiness

Return to [readiness and remediation](/docs/readiness) to see how the check and
accepted proof affect the duty. When your proof comes from a protected
application, [use Runtime records in a Control](/docs/framework-proof) explains
how to define the relevant requests and outcomes. A successful Action or
collected provider fact does not become a passing Test on its own.
